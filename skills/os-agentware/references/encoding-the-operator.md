# Encoding the operator

The hardest part of building a system is not organizing files. It is articulating how the operator actually works, which they have never had to do, because nobody explains themselves to themselves in their inner monologue. The AI is a good interrogator for this. It does not get bored and it does not judge, and explaining yourself turns out to be a skill you can learn.

## Three ways to get it out

**Correction mining.** Seed the instructions with a few pieces of the operator's craft, work a real session, then ask: what did they have to tell me to do differently? Those corrections are exactly the things the AI will not do on its own, and each one is a candidate principle, SOP, or script. This is the channel you will use most, and it is why the first rules in a system come from real sessions rather than from a planning conversation.

**Draft-diff.** When the operator can judge but cannot articulate, hand them two versions. They pick, and you tell them what changed, because all they know is that one is better. Generalize the difference into a principle they can approve or correct. The correction is usually the real principle arriving.

**Generate, then critique.** For taste the operator holds but cannot describe, have a worker with zero context produce the artifact cold. Their reaction to a real thing is far more specific than their opinion from memory, and the reaction is what gets recorded.

## Two traps when writing it down

**Encode the principle, not the phrase.** If you hand a stock skill-builder a writing sample, it pulls phrases out and quotes them in the instructions, and then the system says those exact phrases everywhere. The operator's voice is not "what's up y'all." It is a certain vernacular, a certain casualness, a certain positivity, a willingness to use contractions. Describe the effect the phrasing should have and let the model find the words. Keep a verbatim phrase only when the phrase itself is the craft, and say so, so it is read as a sample and not a script.

**Capture verbatim material as it happens.** Anything the operator says mid-session that carries craft, a decision, or an idea goes into a file before the thread ends. Working threads die with their context, and a summary written later is already the model's words instead of theirs. When the system's own files start to sound like a model, going back to the operator's captured words is the repair.

## When the operator is a strong practitioner

Their job in the conversation is to contribute what the AI cannot infer from training data: their craft, their process, their intuition about where things break. The system's job is to make that contribution easy and well-timed. Ask the question that draws out what they know, frame the choice that exposes their judgment, push back on the move that contradicts how they think. The point is to pull the operator's genius out, not to generate output for them to rubber-stamp. If every operator lets the AI do everything the default way, everything ends up the same.
